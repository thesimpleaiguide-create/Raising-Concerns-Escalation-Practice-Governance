Safeguarding Governance • Escalation & Practice Assurance
01

Duty to Intervene

Active Safeguard: Chaperones must not remain passive witnesses. If a patient shows acute distress, pain, or unconsented touch occurs, the chaperone must intervene immediately.

Immediate Action: Pause the examination politely, check on the patient's well-being, and advocate for procedural adjustments or cessation.

02

Reporting & Escalation

Internal Escalation: Concerns regarding clinician conduct or boundary breaches must be reported to the Practice Safeguarding Lead or Caldicott Guardian.

Whistleblowing Protection: Staff are legally protected under the Public Interest Disclosure Act and supported by Freedom to Speak Up Guardians.

03

Registers & Annual Audits

Central Chaperone Register: Practices must maintain an up-to-date register detailing trained staff names, accredited training completion, and current DBS checks.

CQC Assurance: Conduct annual clinical audits reviewing chaperone offer compliance rates, documentation standards, and patient feedback.

!

Escalation & Whistleblowing Pathways

Protecting patients, staff, and safety standards

Staff members who raise genuine concerns regarding clinical safety, boundaries, or patient safeguarding are protected under the Public Interest Disclosure Act (PIDA) 1998.

The 3-Tier Escalation Pathway

Tier 1: Internal Practice Escalation

Report directly to the Practice Safeguarding Lead, Caldicott Guardian, or Practice Manager immediately following the consultation. Record factual contemporaneous notes.

Tier 2: Freedom to Speak Up Guardian (FTSUG)

If you feel unable to raise the concern within the practice, contact the local Integrated Care Board (ICB) Freedom to Speak Up Guardian for confidential, independent advice and escalation.

Tier 3: External Regulators

Contact the Care Quality Commission (CQC), General Medical Council (GMC), or Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) if internal routes fail to resolve urgent patient safety risks.
